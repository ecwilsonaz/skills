# BRIEF — lossy compression for executive communication

The third mode. Unlike ARCHITECT and POLISH, BRIEF is **deliberately lossy**:
selection is the point, and thoroughness is spent to buy the reader's attention.
The contract changes accordingly:

- **Retained facts stay sacred.** Anything that survives must be true, attributed,
  and unspun. No manufactured implication — compression is where implication-forging
  is MOST tempting (two survivors seated together imply a link the source never drew).
- **Omission is legal but visible.** Every substantive cut goes in the CUT LEDGER
  (below). The editor proposes the loss; the author owns it.
- **Tone outranks grammatical completeness — never clarity.** Fragments and
  telegraphic constructions are licensed when the register fits (Smart Brevity's
  write-like-you-talk; Strunk's ear-over-handbook and compensating-merit escape
  valve). A shortcut that costs comprehension is a false economy — unexplained
  initialisms and cryptic pith waste more reader time than they save.

Method distilled from: VandeHei/Allen/Schwartz *Smart Brevity*, McCormack *Brief*,
Bernoff *Writing Without Bullshit*, Strunk (*Elements of Style*, 1918 core),
Bryar & Carr *Working Backwards*, and military BLUF doctrine (AR 25-50). Ideas
with attribution; the prose and examples here are the skill's own.

## 0 · Intake — negotiate the frame before cutting

Entry is always opt-in: an explicit trigger phrase, or the user accepting one of
SKILL.md's built-in offers (at Anchor for a workplace-directed artifact, or after
POLISH converges above its frame). Never run BRIEF unasked.

Ask (or infer and state) three things:

1. **Reader and delta.** Who exactly reads this, and what do they already know?
   Include only the delta — never re-teach. An expert reader gets zero background.
2. **Time budget → length cap.** Size by reader-minutes, not content volume:
   an email one screen / ≤250 words; a manager's broadcast ≤400; a summary item
   ≤200 (the engagement cliff); an exec summary of a long document = a trailer,
   not a digest. The cap is a forcing function — no cramming tricks; the cap
   exists to force the cut.
3. **Register.** Writing TO an executive (respectful recommendation, upward) or
   AS one (candor, mission-tie, no lawyered spin)? Fragments allowed, or formal?

## 1 · The spine — BLUF Core

Assemble in this order. The first three elements ARE the message; everything
after is optional service.

1. **Tease** (≤6 words) — subject line/heading. Specific, urgent, accurate; no
   cleverness. The ask itself when there is one.
2. **Lede** (1 sentence, ≤ a dozen words) — the single thing the reader must
   remember, declarative. Mine your draft's ENDING for it: the writer's last
   paragraph usually contains the point, discovered while drafting. Move it up.
3. **Why it matters** (1–2 sentences, bolded label) — additive significance,
   never a restatement. The most commonly omitted element; never skip it.
4. **Support** (≤3 points, bulleted if ≥3) — ranked by importance; strongest
   first, each opening on a strong word. Keep Level-1 detail, spend Level-2
   sparingly, kill Level-3 entirely.
5. **Ask + deadline** — explicit action, named owner, date. A message with no
   requested action and no news shouldn't be sent. If the source states no date,
   don't derive or invent one (even by arithmetic from stated facts — that's an
   added claim); flag the missing deadline in the Ask for the author to supply.
6. **Go deeper** (optional) — a link or appendix pointer. Cut to the appendix,
   not to oblivion: relocation proves brevity didn't cost rigor.

**Pre-answer** the two or three objections the reader will fire back — one line
each, at the end or in the appendix. It saves a round-trip and proves the
thinking is done.

## 2 · Compression operations (applied in order)

- **SO-WHAT-FILTER** — every sentence must answer "so what?" for THIS reader or
  die. The writer chooses what matters; never serve a buffet.
- **DELTA-ONLY** — cut everything the reader already knows: background, history,
  credentials, methodology, the path by which you figured it out.
- **BAD-NEWS-FIRST** — the problem goes in the tease/lede, who's responsible and
  actions taken next, reassurance (with a real number) at the end, where it's
  earned. Burying it reads as spin and the reader finds it anyway.
- **HALVE-TWICE** — cut the draft to half its length; then do it again. Done
  compressing? You're halfway.
- **HEDGE-DELETE** — hedging auxiliaries and weasel qualifiers go. Make the
  boldest defensible statement; if you might actually be wrong, don't weaken the
  claim — drop it or state the uncertainty as fact ("unconfirmed; verifying by
  Friday").
- **NUMBER-FOR-ADJECTIVE** — replace evaluative adjectives with a figure plus
  context (comparison or base), ≤3 significant digits. A metric beats an
  adjective; a recommendation beats a description; a deadline beats a hope.
- **POSITIVE-FORM** — state what is, not what is not: "did not pass" → "failed."
  Denial only for real antithesis.
- **TELEGRAPH** — where register allows, compress to fragments: "Revenue: down
  12%. Cause: churn, not pricing. Ask: approve the retention hire." Labels +
  payloads. Grammar yields to tone here — by design.
- **END-STRONG** — the emphatic slot is the end of the sentence and of the
  document; land on the payload or the ask, never on a qualifier.
- **STOP** — no summary of the summary, no warm-down, no cheery closing. Say the
  thing; stop.

**Final sweep.** After assembling the artifact and before presenting the ledger,
run ONE declutter pass over it using the operations.md catalog (Tier 1–2) — a
single sweep, not the POLISH round loop, and under the BRIEF gate, not the POLISH
gate: anchor on the BRIEF artifact itself for clutter, consulting the true
original only for facts and attributions. This catches clutter introduced while
composing; it must not restore cut content or relitigate the ledger.

## 3 · The BRIEF gate (adversarial, every pass)

Against the true original, check:

- [ ] Every retained fact, number, and attribution accurate and unspun; reported
  opinions still attributed (a compressed "she says X is broken" must not become
  "X is broken").
- [ ] **No manufactured implication by survivorship**: did cutting the middle
  seat two facts into a causal/ironic adjacency the source never asserted?
- [ ] **CUT LEDGER complete**: every dropped substantive item listed in one line
  each. The reader of the ledger can veto any cut.
- [ ] **Stands alone**: the artifact works without its author present, and can
  serve later as the record. If it needs oral accompaniment, it's over-compressed.
- [ ] **Clarity caps compression**: no unexplained initialism, no pith that needs
  a decoder; if the reader must ask a clarifying question, the compression failed.
- [ ] **The "why" and the "how" survived** in proportion to the reader's
  expertise: deep brevity presumes the reader can unpack it; for a reader without
  the context, an over-compressed message is confusion, not concision.
- [ ] **Curtness check**: if it now reads cold or robotic, restore one breath of
  voice — a dab, not a dollop. Directness and sensitivity coexist.

## 4 · When NOT to use BRIEF

- **Complex, contested, multivariate decisions.** When the reasoning is causal
  and interconnected and the reader must audit it, fragmentation destroys exactly
  the information that matters: bullets flatten importance, sever connections,
  and let half-baked thinking hide. Write connected prose under a hard cap
  instead (the six-pager stance), and run POLISH, not BRIEF. Rule of thumb:
  compress *status, requests, and conclusions*; never compress *the argument
  itself* when the argument is the deliverable.
- **Case-building persuasion** where the ask dies without the windup.
- **Emotionally loaded messages** (personal critique, layoffs) — directness yes,
  telegraphic compression no; these need deliberate emotional acknowledgment.
- **Reader lacks the expertise to unpack it** — expand within discipline instead.
- **Detail-obligatory containers** (contracts, compliance, specs): relocate
  detail, never delete it.

## 5 · Output format

```
BRIEF ─────────────────────────────────────────
FRAME  reader: <who> · cap: <words/format> · register: <to-exec | as-exec | team>

[the compressed artifact]

CUT LEDGER
  − <dropped item, one line>            [veto to restore]
  − <dropped item, one line>
  → appendix: <items relocated, not cut>

Integrity: retained facts verified · attributions intact · no implication added
by adjacency · stands alone · why-it-matters present
```

At the ledger, STOP for the author's review before treating the compression as
final — cuts are proposals, exactly as Phase-1 forks are.
